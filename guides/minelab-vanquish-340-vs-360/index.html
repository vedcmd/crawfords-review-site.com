Minelab Vanquish 340 vs 360: The £60 Question Answered

Vanquish 340 vs 360 compared for UK detectorists — search modes, the Equinox-matching Target ID scale, 5m waterproofing and batteries. Which entry Minelab to buy.

Published 28 November 2026 · By the Crawfords Metal Detectors team

The two cheapest routes into Minelab's Multi-IQ technology sit close enough in price that the choice deserves a proper answer. Here is the one we give across the counter.

Start with what they share

Both the Vanquish 340 and Vanquish 360 run genuine Multi-IQ simultaneous multi-frequency — the same family of technology as the Equinox and X-Terra Elite, and the single most consequential spec on either sheet. Neither is a single-frequency machine in marketing dress. That shared brain is why the 340 is still worth buying, and why the differences are about refinement, not capability.

Modes: why "Park" is a trap on UK ground

The 340 offers Coin, Jewellery and All Metal; the 360 renames the game with Park, Beach and All Metal — location-based modes, matching the convention on every higher Minelab. One warning matters more than the rest: Park mode rejects Target IDs 1–20 to kill foil in municipal parks — exactly the band where hammered coins live. On UK fields, run All Metal as your default on either machine; use Beach for trashy ground and the coast.

The upgrade that actually teaches you

The 340 reads targets on a 50-number scale (-9 to +40). The 360 reads on -19 to 99 — the identical scale the Equinox series uses. Finer resolution means a Victorian penny and a modern coin stop landing on the same number — and every ID you memorise on a 360 transfers straight to an Equinox 900 when you upgrade. Two seasons of ID memory move with you.

Water, pinpoint, power

The 360 is fully submersible to 5m (IP68); the 340's coil is waterproof but its control box is not — it's a land machine that can get its coil wet. The 360 adds a Pinpoint mode (tidier holes from day one; the 340 has none) and an internal rechargeable battery good for about 10 hours, against the 340's four AAs — which, in fairness, a corner shop can revive mid-dig.

Our answer

If the budget genuinely stops at the 340, it is no mistake — same engine, honest machine. But for anyone staying in the hobby past a season, the 360's waterproofing, pinpoint and Equinox-matching IDs are worth the stretch, and the 360 Starter Bundle packages it with the essentials.
